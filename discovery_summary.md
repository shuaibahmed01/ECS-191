# Discovery Summary

## Overview  
We ran 10 discovery interviews with UC Davis students to understand how people actually keep track of school. The biggest takeaway: students are not lacking tools, they are drowning in too many of them. Information is scattered across Canvas, Gradescope, email, Discord, syllabi, and personal planners, so everyone ends up building their own system just to stay organized.

We originally thought the main problem was class communication and group chats. After these interviews, our thinking shifted. The real issue is fragmentation and the lack of a single reliable place to understand what is going on across classes, both current and future.

## Key Problems

### Fragmented information  
Students have to check multiple platforms to understand one class. Each professor uses a different combination of tools, so deadlines, updates, and policies are scattered. Many described Canvas as a scavenger hunt. Even organized students end up manually copying everything into Notion or Google Calendar at the start of each quarter just to make sense of it.

### Constant app switching  
Students rotate between Canvas, email, Gradescope, Discord, and other tools daily. Logging in repeatedly is annoying, and missing one platform or one email can mean missing an assignment or quiz. Some students have developed routines to check everything every morning just to avoid falling behind.

### Manual planning overhead  
A lot of people rebuild their planning system every quarter. They export deadlines into calendars or Notion and maintain it manually. Canvas exists in its own silo and does not integrate well with how students actually plan their time across school, work, and life.

### Unreliable class group chats  
Class Discords are helpful but messy. Links get buried in Canvas messages, expire, or are impossible to find if you join late. Some students miss out on resources entirely because they cannot access the group chat.

### Uncertainty about future classes  
One thing we did not expect: students struggle to find reliable info about classes they want to take. Prerequisites, course changes, and enrollment requirements are often unclear. Advisors take time to respond, and there is no central place to see what students in the class are experiencing right now.

## Early Ideas

- A single hub that aggregates deadlines, announcements, and key info across platforms  
- Automatic extraction of assignments and dates into a unified timeline  
- A persistent class group chat that is always accessible  
- A forum where students can share info about classes for future quarters  

## Takeaways

- The main pain is fragmentation, not lack of information  
- Students spend time consolidating tools instead of using them  
- Missing one message or platform can have real consequences  
- Future class planning is a surprisingly stressful gap  

Overall, there is strong potential for a product that acts as a central academic hub and gives students one clear picture of what is going on.
