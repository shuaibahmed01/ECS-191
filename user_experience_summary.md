# User Experience Summary

We ran 10 lightweight user interviews with UC Davis students after letting them try CourseHub. The goal was to understand whether the core idea (AI syllabus agent + class chat + centralized course info) actually solves real problems students have with Canvas, and what would prevent them from adopting it.

Overall, the problems we’re targeting are real, but switching behavior is much harder than expected. Students already have strong workflows built around Canvas, Discord, ChatGPT, Google Calendar, and personal systems. CourseHub was seen as interesting and occasionally useful, but not yet strong enough to replace or meaningfully augment those tools.


## What people liked

The **Course Agent / syllabus AI** was consistently the strongest feature. Multiple users said it was helpful to quickly answer questions like office hours, exam dates, grading breakdowns, or late policies without scrolling through a long syllabus PDF.

Some specific examples:

- One student realized they had the **wrong finals date** after checking the AI and had to adjust their schedule. This was actually a positive moment because it prevented them from preparing for the wrong day.
- Another user said the agent saved them from scrolling through a **9 page syllabus** and immediately pulled out exam dates and office hours.
- Several users liked that the AI answers included **citations from the syllabus**, which increased trust.

In general, the value proposition that resonated most was: “I can just ask a question instead of digging through Canvas or a syllabus PDF.” The **all-in-one idea** also resonated conceptually. Some users liked the idea of having:

- syllabus
- class chat
- AI assistant
- schedules

all inside a single class space.

One user described it as feeling like a combination of **Discord + calendar + ChatGPT**, which they thought was more organized than Canvas. Another user liked the class-scoped group chat idea because their iMessage group broke the night before an assignment due date and Discord servers sometimes get cluttered with unrelated messages.

## What didn’t work well

### Switching Costs

Most students already have a working system that looks something like: Canvas + Discord + ChatGPT + Google Calendar + personal notes system. Because of that, CourseHub often felt like **an extra step rather than a replacement**.

Examples:

- One student already pastes their syllabus into **ChatGPT** and asks questions there.
- Another has a **Notion system they've built over 2 years** and said they wouldn’t abandon it for a new app.
- Many said they would just **command+F the syllabus PDF** instead of uploading it.

A second major issue was **manual setup friction**. Several users disliked that they had to:

- manually add classes
- upload syllabi
- upload slides

especially when that information already exists in Canvas. One person described it as: “basically doing double the work” since they already set up their schedule in Canvas and Google Calendar. Another user said it felt pointless to upload slides when professors already post them on Canvas.

### Network effects 

The **group chat feature had almost no value** for most users because nobody else was on the app. Multiple interviews mentioned this directly:

- group chats were empty
- friends already use Discord
- nobody wants to be the first person asking everyone to download another app

One user described it as a classic **chicken and egg problem**. Another said the chat idea solves a real issue (expired Discord links from Canvas inbox), but it only works if the whole class is using it.

### Coverage problems

One interview revealed a major usability gap. The student was a **biochemistry major** and almost all the classes in the app were CS courses. They could only add **1 out of their 4 classes**, which made the app basically unusable for them. Their reaction was: “the concept is good but it needs to have all departments” Without broad class coverage, the value of the product drops quickly.

### Trust and AI concerns

Accuracy anxiety wasn’t the main issue. Students generally trusted the AI **when it clearly cited the syllabus**. However, several concerns came up:

- what if the AI misinterprets something
- what if the professor changes the schedule later
- what if the syllabus format changes
- uncertainty about what data is shared vs private

Some users also mentioned privacy concerns when signing in with their UC Davis email or uploading syllabi. Another interesting pattern was **ChatGPT comparison**. Students who already use ChatGPT heavily felt our agent was **less flexible** than prompting ChatGPT directly.

### Canvas is the real competitor

Nearly every interview referenced Canvas. The most common feedback was that the app would be **much more compelling as a Canvas extension** rather than a standalone platform. Reasons included:

- students are already overwhelmed with apps
- Canvas is mandatory so it’s always open
- switching contexts between tools is annoying

One user explicitly said: “it would have to do everything Canvas does plus more” Another said they would use it immediately **if it lived inside Canvas**.

## Quick quantitative snapshot

Across the 10 interviews:

- **7/10 users** found the syllabus AI genuinely useful for quick questions
- **8/10 users** mentioned Canvas as their main existing workflow
- **6/10 users** said uploading syllabi or materials felt like extra work
- **5/10 users** compared the feature directly to ChatGPT
- **6/10 users** said group chat was useless because nobody else was on the platform
- **4/10 users** explicitly said they would prefer a Canvas integration or extension

## Big takeaways

1. The **AI syllabus agent solves a real pain point**. Quickly extracting policies, dates, and office hours from long syllabi is valuable.

2. The biggest barrier is **workflow replacement**. Students already have a working stack of tools and are reluctant to switch.

3. **Manual setup is too heavy.** Uploading syllabi or slides when Canvas already has them creates friction.

4. **Network effects matter for chat.** Without classmates already using the app, the feature has zero value.

5. The strongest strategic insight is that **Canvas is the platform we actually need to integrate with**, not compete against.

Right now CourseHub is interesting, but not essential. The product needs deeper integration, less manual setup, and clearer differentiation from both Canvas and ChatGPT before students would realistically adopt it in their daily workflow.
